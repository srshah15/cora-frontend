CORA · Clinical Operations Research Assistant

Quick-start guide

CORA reads a clinical research protocol and drafts an operational plan: the visit schedule, procedures at each visit, staffing, safety reporting, billing, and pharmacy, equipment and technology needs. Everything it produces is a draft for your team to check.

Download this guide as a PDF

Public protocols only. Upload protocols that are publicly available, for example from ClinicalTrials.gov. Don't upload confidential sponsor or institutional documents, or anything with patient information.

For coordinators

1. Upload a protocol

  1. Drag a protocol PDF onto Drop a protocol PDF here, or click it to choose a file. PDFs up to 50 MB and 150 pages work.
  2. Wait on the processing screen. It shows each step; most protocols finish within two minutes.
  3. Just exploring? Click Try a sample protocol to see a finished plan built from fictional data.

2. Read the plan

  • Each category has its own section. Use the list on the left to jump to one.
  • Choose your role under View as to put your sections first: coordinators see the visit schedule and procedures, project managers see staffing and safety, and operations leaders see technology, pharmacy and equipment. Nothing is hidden.
  • The visit schedule shows each visit with its timing and window. The procedures grid marks which visits each procedure happens at.

3. Understand confidence and flags

  • Every item shows a confidence level and the protocol page it came from (for example p. 19).
  • Items below 70% confidence are highlighted, with a note explaining what to check. Sections show 3 to review, Needs check or Looks good.
  • Turn on Only show flagged items to see just what needs attention.
  • A Limited extraction banner means CORA's AI step wasn't available and the plan came from a simpler keyword search. Review it carefully.

4. Export and start over

  • Download PDF or Download Word saves the plan, including review decisions. Both are marked as drafts for review.
  • New protocol clears the current plan and your decisions from the screen, so export first.

For reviewers

1. Open the review queue

  1. On the plan, click Review N flagged.
  2. Type your name in the Reviewer box at the top. It's recorded with every decision and printed in the PDF's sign-off block.

2. Check each item against the protocol

  • The Source protocol pane shows the page the item came from. Use Open in new tab for a larger view.
  • Read CORA's note under the confidence bar: it says why the item was flagged.

3. Decide

  • Accept if it matches the protocol.
  • Fix any field, then click Accept with edits.
  • Reject if the item isn't a real requirement.
  • Add an optional Comment explaining a change or rejection. Undo decision reopens an item.
  • Shortcuts: A accept, R reject, ← → previous and next item.

4. Sign off

  1. When the screen says All flagged items reviewed, click Download PDF.
  2. Check the unflagged items too: a high confidence level doesn't guarantee an item is correct.
  3. Print the PDF and complete the Reviewer sign-off block (name, signature, date) before the plan is used.

Frequently asked questions

Why was this item flagged?
CORA's confidence was below 70%, usually because the protocol is ambiguous, the information is spread across several places, or a table was hard to read. The note on the item says which. Check it against the source page, then accept, edit or reject it.
Why is a category empty?
Either the protocol doesn't cover it, or CORA couldn't find it; the section's note says what to check. Billing is always empty for now, because CORA doesn't yet work out who pays for each item. If you know the protocol covers a category, add it to the plan yourself after export.
Is my protocol saved anywhere?
The PDF and its text are never stored. If your site has turned on saved plans, the extracted plan and review decisions are kept so the same protocol opens instantly next time; otherwise nothing is kept after you leave the page.
What happens if the AI part is down?
CORA still produces a plan using a simpler keyword search and shows a Limited extraction banner explaining why. That plan has fewer details and more gaps, so review it carefully, or try again later.
Can I upload a scanned paper protocol?
Yes. CORA reads scanned pages with text recognition (OCR), which is slower and less accurate than a PDF with selectable text. Scans with more than 50 scanned pages are refused, and handwriting or poor-quality scans won't read well.
Can I change the plan after exporting it?
Yes. While the plan is still open, go back to the review queue, use Undo decision to change a flagged item, and export again. The Word file can also be edited directly. Each export is a snapshot; it doesn't update itself.
Does CORA replace reviewing the protocol?
No. CORA is a starting point that saves time. Qualified study staff must check the plan against the protocol and sign it off before it's used.

What each category means

Visit schedule
Each visit, with its timing and allowed window.
Procedures
What happens at each visit, from the Schedule of Activities.
Staffing
Roles the protocol names, with qualifications if it states them.
Safety reporting
What to report, to whom, and how fast.
Billing
Sponsor-paid vs. standard-of-care items. Not filled in yet.
Equipment
Devices and supplies the protocol requires.
Pharmacy
Study drug storage, preparation and accountability.
Technology
Systems the site must use, such as EDC or IWRS.

How the confidence cutoff works

CORA scores each item from 0 to 100% for how sure it is. Anything under 70% is flagged and goes into the review queue. The cutoff is a setting your administrator can change.

What keyword fallback is

If the AI step can't run (no connection, an error, or it returns nothing), CORA searches the text for known terms and table layouts instead. You still get a plan, under a Limited extraction banner, but with fewer details and more gaps.

What CORA can't do yet

  • Work out billing designations or estimate staff effort (FTE).
  • Read protocols over 150 pages, scans with more than 50 scanned pages, or password-protected PDFs.
  • Edit items that weren't flagged; change those in the Word export.
